# Course content and exam alignment

## Course terminology

Preserve course notation and terminology when changing them could confuse the learner or cost marks. Check symbols and overloaded terms against their definitions in the source. For numeric exercises, verify that every stated point satisfies its equation before working an answer; if it does not, flag the inconsistency instead of guessing a correction. A compact glossary is useful when terms are unfamiliar or when slides, exams, and the learner’s language differ. Explain alternate terminology where it helps connect ideas; do not silently replace course-specific terms.

## Exam alignment

Use available exams to identify recurring topics, task formats, and question wording. A bilingual keyword list, formula sheet, recipe bank, or theory-question bank may help when supported by the materials or requested. Only quote question stems and report frequencies after checking the papers. Treat student summaries, solved guides, and spreadsheets as secondary evidence: recheck copied inputs, arithmetic, signs, and assumptions against the original exercise before teaching their results. A worked answer can carry a transcription error even when its method is sound.

Extracting PDF text is useful for searching and counting, but it can lose diagrams, plots, and page layout. Inspect rendered pages for visual questions, graphs, and tables before deciding that a topic is absent.

## Practice and retrieval

Worked examples, partially completed steps, error analysis, quizzes, and mixed practice can help learners apply material. Choose methods that fit the topic, the exam format, and the learner’s available time. Do not require every technique in every unit. Label invented examples as practice rather than past exam questions, and verify answers against the source.
